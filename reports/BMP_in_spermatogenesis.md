## Executive Summary

Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) play multiple essential roles in mammalian spermatogenesis, from primordial germ cell (PGC) specification to adult spermatogonial stem cell (SSC) maintenance, spermatogonial differentiation, and spermiogenesis [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2017.03.002)[[2]](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.00650)[[5]](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.aal1910). BMP4, produced by Sertoli cells, acts as a key paracrine factor that promotes both proliferation and differentiation of spermatogonia through the ALK3/SMAD5 pathway [[2]](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.00650)[[3]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2004.08.034). BMP8A uniquely activates both SMAD1/5/8 and SMAD2/3 to sustain spermatogenesis [[5]](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.aal1910). BMP signaling is tightly regulated by inhibitory SMADs (SMAD6, SMAD7), extracellular modifiers (SMOC1/2), and E3 ubiquitin ligases (Smurf) [[8]](https://doi.org/10.1002/dvdy.21401)[[15]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gep.2024.119383)[[9]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2013.08.014), and its dysregulation is associated with male infertility including non-obstructive azoospermia [[6]](#ref6).

## Detailed Answer

### 1. BMP4 as a Master Regulator of Spermatogonial Differentiation

**BMP4 is produced by Sertoli cells and drives spermatogonial differentiation** [[2]](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.00650).

Pellegrini et al. (2003) first identified the BMP4/ALK3/SMAD5 signaling pathway in mouse prepubertal testis [[2]](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.00650). BMP4 is produced by Sertoli cells early in postnatal life and is progressively downregulated in peri-pubertal Sertoli cells. Its receptor ALK3 (BMPR1A) and the R-Smad SMAD5 are specifically expressed in both proliferating PGCs and postnatal spermatogonia. BMP4 stimulation of cultured spermatogonia induces SMAD4/5 nuclear translocation and formation of a DNA-binding complex with p300/CBP. In vitro, BMP4 exerts both mitogenic and differentiative effects, inducing [3H]thymidine incorporation and Kit expression, thereby rendering Kit-negative spermatogonia sensitive to Stem Cell Factor (SCF) [[2]](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.00650).

**Bmp4 haploinsufficiency causes germ cell degeneration and reduced fertility** [[3]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2004.08.034).

Hu et al. (2004) demonstrated that Bmp4 heterozygous males on a C57BL/6 background exhibit compromised fertility due to germ cell degeneration, reduced sperm counts, and decreased sperm motility. Notably, Bmp7, Bmp8a, and Bmp8b (60A class) and Bmp4 (Dpp class) have distinct region-specific requirements: Bmp7/8 mutants affect caput and cauda epididymis, whereas Bmp4 heterozygotes show degeneration specifically in the corpus epididymis [[3]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2004.08.034).

**Vitamin A/retinoid signaling represses BMP4 expression in germ cells** [[4]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2005.07.009).

Baleato et al. (2005) showed that BMP4 is expressed in adult germ cells, particularly spermatogonia, at both mRNA and protein levels. BMP4 expression is significantly upregulated in testes of vitamin A-deficient (VAD) mice and downregulated by retinol (but not retinoic acid). Germ cells utilize the BMP4 intron 2 promoter in addition to the classical 1A and 1B promoters. Retinoid-mediated suppression of BMP4 through the 1A and intron 2 promoters is required for resumption of spermatogenesis in VAD animals [[4]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2005.07.009).

### 2. BMP8A: A Dual-SMAD-Pathway Ligand in Spermatogenesis

**BMP8A uniquely activates both SMAD1/5/8 and SMAD2/3 pathways in spermatogonia** [[5]](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.aal1910).

Wu et al. (2017) reported that while most BMPs signal exclusively through SMAD1/5/8, BMP8A is exceptional in activating both SMAD1/5/8 and SMAD2/3. In undifferentiated mouse spermatogonia, BMP8A activates SMAD1/5/8 via ALK3-ACVR2A/BMPR2 receptor complexes and SMAD2/3 via ALK5-ACVR2A/ACVR2B/TGFBR2 complexes. SMAD2/3 signaling promotes germ cell proliferation, while SMAD1/5/8 directs subsequent spermatogonial differentiation. BMP8A promoted spermatogenesis in cultured testis explants, producing functionally competent spermatids for fertilization [[5]](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.aal1910). Mutation in Bmp8a or Bmp8b causes postnatal depletion of spermatogonia in mice, and Bmp8a (but not Bmp8b) is predominantly expressed in neonatal spermatogonia [[5]](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.aal1910). A recent review by Tang et al. (2023) further summarizes BMP-8a's role in late spermatogenesis, involving SMAD2/3 and SMAD1/5/8 pathways [[13]](https://doi.org/10.1080/03008207.2022.2160326).

### 3. SMAD Expression and BMP Signaling Machinery in the Testis

**BMP signaling is regulated by cell-specific expression of SMAD proteins** [[8]](https://doi.org/10.1002/dvdy.21401).

Itman & Loveland (2008) systematically characterized SMAD expression in the mouse testis. SMAD1, SMAD5, SMAD8, SMAD4, SMAD6, and SMAD7 show ubiquitous expression during testis development but become cell-specific in the adult. SMAD6 protein is regulated at the onset of spermatogenesis, suggesting differential BMP responsiveness among spermatogonial subpopulations. In vitro, immature Sertoli cells and spermatogonia transduce BMP2 and BMP4 signals through SMAD1, SMAD5, and SMAD8 [[8]](https://doi.org/10.1002/dvdy.21401).

Ciller et al. (2016) quantified expression of all BMP family members in postnatal mouse testis: BMP2, BMP3, BMP3b, BMP4, BMP5, BMP6, BMP7, BMP8a, BMP8b, BMP15, and receptors BMPR1A, BMPR1B, BMPR2 are all expressed at all stages screened (weeks 2, 4, 6, 8), with levels varying by age and developmental stage [[14]](https://doi.org/10.33549/physiolres.933193).

**SMOC1/SMOC2 are BMP signal modifiers with distinct testicular expression patterns** [[15]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gep.2024.119383).

Ono et al. (2024) characterized Smoc1 and Smoc2, SPARC-family BMP signal modifiers downstream of TGF-¦Â/FGF/PDGF. SMOC1 is highly expressed in male germ cells, while SMOC2 expression switches from testicular somatic cells at neonatal/juvenile stages to germ cells in adulthood, suggesting distinct roles in reproductive development [[15]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gep.2024.119383).

### 4. BMP4 in Human Sertoli Cells and Male Infertility

**BMP4 is the first identified autocrine factor regulating human Sertoli cell proliferation** [[6]](#ref6).

Hai et al. (2015) demonstrated that BMP4 and its receptors are present in adult human Sertoli cells. BMP4 promotes DNA synthesis and proliferation of Sertoli cells via SMAD1/5 phosphorylation and ID2/ID3 upregulation, while BMP4 antagonist noggin and BMP4 knockdown reduce Sertoli cell division. BMP4 knockdown also inhibits synthesis of FGF2, SCF, ZO-1, and claudin 11, and enhances p27kip1 expression. Tissue arrays revealed that BMP4 overexpression may be associated with Sertoli cell-only syndrome and maturation arrest in non-obstructive azoospermia patients [[6]](#ref6).

Ma et al. (2023) confirmed in Tibetan sheep that BMP4 overexpression in Sertoli cells upregulates SMAD5, BMPR1A, BMPR1B, and tight junction proteins (Claudin11, Occludin, ZO1), promoting cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis through Bcl2 upregulation, Caspase3/Bax downregulation, and CyclinA2/CDK2 upregulation [[16]](https://doi.org/10.1093/jas/skac393).

### 5. BMP Signaling in Primordial Germ Cells and Meiotic Entry

**BMP signaling is required for PGC specification (inductive mode) in mammals** [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2017.03.002).

Lochab & Extavour (2017) comprehensively reviewed BMP signaling across metazoan reproductive systems. They concluded that BMP signaling is required in the germ line across all life stages in bilaterians that use inductive signaling for PGC specification, with the exception of species using germ plasm (preformation) [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2017.03.002). BMP4 is the most broadly employed ligand; BMP8 is restricted to spermatogenesis, while BMP6 and BMP15 have roles restricted to oogenesis [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2017.03.002).

**Somatic cell-derived BMPs induce premature meiosis in male germ cells via DAZL** [[10]](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202101585R).

Zhang et al. (2022) showed that conditional ¦Â-catenin activation in Sertoli cells causes premature meiosis in male embryonic germ cells. The BMP signaling pathway was enriched in transcriptome analysis after ¦Â-catenin activation. BMP pathway inhibition with LDN-193189 rescued this phenotype, revealing that BMP signaling mediates meiosis initiation through DNA methylation changes (reduced DNMT3A) and upregulation of DAZL [[10]](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202101585R).

In zebrafish, BMP-SMAD1/9 signaling is critical for PGC maintenance rather than fate determination, promoting PGC proliferation and preventing apoptosis via suppression of DNA damage response pathways (ATR-pCHK1) [[17]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70624-8).

### 6. BMP Signaling in the Drosophila Testis: GSC Maintenance and Spermiogenesis

**Smurf attenuates BMP response to control stem cell number and testis growth** [[9]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2013.08.014).

Chang et al. (2013) showed that in Drosophila testis, BMP signaling is spatially restricted and temporally downregulated during development. The HECT-domain E3 ligase Smurf mediates pMad downregulation in GSCs, which is required for both the normal decrease in stem cell number during pupal maturation and for limiting spermatogonia divisions to four rounds [[9]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2013.08.014).

**Soma-to-germline BMP (Gbb/Sax) is essential for spermiogenesis** [[11]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2024.09.016).

Beard et al. (2025) identified that the BMP ligand Glass bottom boat (Gbb), secreted from somatic cyst cells, signals through the type I receptor Saxophone (Sax) in germ cells to maintain proper spermiogenesis. Knockdown leads to aberrant mitochondrial morphology, defective nebenkern formation, and sperm head bundling defects [[11]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2024.09.016).

**ADAR regulates spermatogenesis via BMP signaling modulation** [[12]](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25115643).

Zhang et al. (2024) showed that ADAR (adenosine deaminase acting on RNA) mutations cause male infertility in Drosophila, with deletion causing accumulation of transit-amplifying cells. Transcriptome analysis revealed that ADAR maintains early spermatogenesis homeostasis through the BMP signaling pathway [[12]](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25115643).

### 7. BMP vs TGF-¦Â in the Spermatogonial Niche: Antagonistic Roles

In the zebrafish spermatogonial niche, BMP and TGF-¦Â signaling exert antagonistic roles [[18]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gene.2023.148082). Fernandes da Costa et al. (2024) demonstrated that pharmacological TGF-¦Â inhibition (A83-01) potentiates FSH-mediated spermatogonial differentiation, while BMP inhibition (DMH1) blocks it, increasing undifferentiated spermatogonia and pluripotency gene expression (nanog, pou5f3). BMP signaling thus promotes spermatogonial differentiation, whereas TGF-¦Â maintains the undifferentiated state [[18]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gene.2023.148082).

### 8. BMP4 in Somatic Testicular Cells

**BMP4 inhibits stem/progenitor Leydig cell development** [[19]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41419-022-05471-8).

Li et al. (2022) showed that BMP4 is expressed in the Leydig cell lineage and blocks stem/progenitor Leydig cell entry into adult Leydig cells through both SMAD-dependent (SMAD1/5 phosphorylation, SMAD4) and SMAD-independent (ERK1/2, AMPK) pathways. BMP4 reduced androgen production, downregulated steroidogenesis genes (Lhcgr, Scarb1, Cyp11a1, Hsd11b1, Insl3), and inhibited stem Leydig cell proliferation in vitro and regeneration in vivo [[19]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41419-022-05471-8).

**BMP4-SNAI2 signaling in testicular germ cell tumours** [[20]](https://doi.org/10.1111/andr.12823).

Micati et al. (2020) showed that BMP4 (but not activin A) upregulates SNAI2 (~18-fold) in seminoma TCam-2 cells via IPO5-mediated nuclear translocation. SNAI2 reduction inhibited BMP4-induced cell survival, suggesting therapeutic potential for targeting BMP4/SNAIL signaling in metastatic testicular germ cell tumours [[20]](https://doi.org/10.1111/andr.12823).

Queries used: `BMP spermatogenesis`, `BMP signaling spermatogonia`, `BMP4 testis`, `BMP male germ cell differentiation` across PubMed, Europe PMC, and Semantic Scholar. A total of 95 unique articles were returned after deduplication. The highest-citation paper (Li et al. 2017, single-cell atlas, 248 citations) was indirectly relevant (BMP mentioned in niche context). Articles focused on non-germline BMP topics (bone, cancer outside testis, odontogenesis) were excluded from core analysis. The Drosophila studies [[9]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2013.08.014)[[11]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2024.09.016)[[12]](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25115643) are included as they provide mechanistic insights conserved across species.

## Limitations & Unverified Claims

- Most functional studies on BMPs in spermatogenesis rely on rodent models and in vitro systems; direct evidence from human in vivo spermatogenesis is limited.
- The relative contributions of different BMP ligands (BMP2, BMP4, BMP7, BMP8A/B) in the adult human seminiferous epithelium are not fully resolved.
- BMP signaling crosstalk with other pathways (Wnt, Notch, FGF) in spermatogonial niche regulation remains incompletely characterized.
- The clinical utility of BMP agonists/antagonists for male infertility treatment is hypothetical at this stage.
